What determines the price?

Five things set most of the price: how many pages and layouts the site has, how many products it sells, how much custom animation it needs, who prepares the content, and which outside services it connects to.

Pages and layouts. Ten pages built on two layouts cost less than five pages that each need their own design. A home page, an about page and a contact page are the usual starting point.

Products and options. A store with 10 products isn't the same job as one with 200. Sizes, colors, bundles and stock per option all add work.

Animation and 3D. A product film that plays as you scroll, or a product you can turn around, is designed and rendered scene by scene. It's often the biggest single part of a quote.

Content. If you send finished text and photos, there's less to prepare. If we write the copy or prepare the images, we'll include that work in the quote.

Connections. A booking engine, Shopify, a newsletter tool or a second language each need setup and testing. We'll check platform compatibility and access before agreeing to an integration.

Costs that come after launch

A website keeps costing a little after it goes live. Plan for these:

Domain. Renewed every year and registered in your name.

Hosting. Paid to the hosting provider. A light, hand-coded site needs less than a heavy theme with many plugins.

Payment fees. Stripe or Shopify charge a fee on each sale. What you pay depends on your country and plan, and it goes to them, not to us.

Apps and plugins. Reviews, subscriptions or shipping tools often have their own monthly plan.

Maintenance and changes. You'll see in your quote whether technical maintenance is required or optional. New pages or content later are quoted when you ask for them.

Website builder or freelance developer?

A website builder is the cheapest way to start. You pay a monthly plan, usually apps on top, and you build it yourself with the builder's layouts.

A freelance developer designs the site around your brand and handles the build, testing and launch. It costs more up front than a builder and should save you time. Ask who'll handle hosting, fixes and changes after launch.

Whichever you choose, count your own hours too. Building and maintaining a site yourself has a cost, even when it isn't on an invoice.

Comparing proposals

Two quotes with the same total can cover very different work. Before you compare prices, check that each proposal lists the pages, products, animations, content and connections it includes, how many revision rounds you'll get, the schedule, and which ongoing costs are required and which are optional.
